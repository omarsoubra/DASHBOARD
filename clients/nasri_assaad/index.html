How This Program Runs
  • WHAT THIS PROGRAM IS FOR You are 191cm and 85kg. You are not overweight - you are undertrained. The goal is not a number on the scale: it is a leaner, more athletic look, muscle kept or built while fat comes off, and lifts that go up. Judge it on the mirror, photos, the tape around your waist and your gym numbers. The scale is one input of several and it will bounce daily - that is water, food and salt, not fat.
  • CALORIES STEP DOWN: 2,500 -> 2,350 -> 2,200 They come down as your body adapts, and protein never moves. The weeks that decide how this ends are 9-12, when food is lowest and you are hungriest - that is when holding your week-8 weights IS the win. If strength drops two sessions running, or sleep and mood go, tell Omar. The answer then is more food, not more discipline.
  • THREE MEALS + A PRE-TRAINING SHAKE, BUILT AROUND YOUR 7PM SESSION: breakfast 7:00am, lunch 1:00pm (prepped, eaten at work), shake 5:30pm on the way to the gym, dinner 8:30pm after you train. The 5:30pm shake is the one people skip and it is the one that decides whether your session is any good - it is liquid so it will not sit heavy. Dinner is deliberately AFTER training.
  • FOOD IS HALAL AND DELIBERATELY REPETITIVE, because that is what you told us you prefer. Every meat line says 'Halal' so you are never guessing at the shops. Salmon and white fish are in there because you said you like fish. All meat and fish weights are RAW; rice and potato are COOKED. Batch-cook Sunday and the week runs itself.
  • FOUR SESSIONS ARE MANDATORY DAY 5 IS OPTIONAL AND MEANS IT. Days 1-4 (Upper A, Lower A, Upper B, Lower B) are a complete week on their own - every muscle gets trained twice. Day 5 is arms, delts and calves only. Nothing important lives on Day 5, so skipping it costs you nothing. Do it only when recovery is good, soreness is manageable, your gym numbers are stable, work has not flattened you and sleep has not been awful. If in doubt, skip it.
  • WEEKS 1-2 ARE RE-ENTRY, NOT TRAINING You are not training at all right now. Two working sets per exercise, 3-4 reps left in the tank, nothing near failure. You should leave the gym feeling like you could have done more. That is the point. Weeks 3-4 go to 3 sets.
  • HOW TO PROGRESS (this is the actual system, not 'lift more'). Every exercise has a rep range, e.g. 8-10. Pick a weight you can do for the BOTTOM of the range at the prescribed effort. Each week try to add reps, not weight. When you hit the TOP of the range on ALL your sets, add weight next session: 2.5kg on upper-body and dumbbell lifts, 5kg on leg press and machines. Then you will drop back to the bottom of the range at the new weight - that is correct, start climbing again. If you cannot hit the bottom of the range for two sessions running, drop the weight 10% and rebuild.
  • EFFORT, IN REPS IN RESERVE (RIR) Weeks 1-2: 3-4 RIR. Weeks 3-8: 2-3 RIR. Weeks 9-12: 1-2 RIR on the main lifts. NEVER take a compound (press, row, leg press, RDL, squat) to failure - the fatigue cost is high and the technique breaks down first. If you want to finish something off, take the LAST set of a cable or machine isolation (lateral raise, curl, pushdown, leg extension) to failure. That is where failure is cheap.
  • REST: 2-3 minutes on the first two exercises of a session, 90 seconds on the middle ones, 60 seconds on isolations. Do not rush the big lifts to save time - short rest costs you reps, and reps are the progress.
  • SESSIONS ARE 45-55 MINUTES Five to six exercises. You train at 7pm after a full day of work; a 90-minute session is how this stops happening by week 5.
  • IF YOU MISS ONE SESSION: do not cram it in. Just continue the rotation on your next gym day. Missing Day 2 means your next session is Day 3, not a double.
  • IF YOU MISS TWO OR THREE DAYS: same answer - pick the rotation up where you left it. The order matters more than the calendar. Never stack two sessions into one day to 'catch up'.
  • IF YOU MISS A WEEK OR MORE: do not walk back in at your old weights. First two sessions back, take 10% off the bar and drop to 2 working sets at 3-4 RIR. You will be back to your previous loads within two weeks, and you will not be crippled for four days.
  • MINIMUM VIABLE WEEK If work or life goes sideways and you can only train twice, do DAY 1 (Upper A) and DAY 2 (Lower A). That covers every major movement pattern - a press, a row, a pulldown, a squat pattern and a hinge. If you can manage three, add Day 3 (Upper B). Two good sessions is a real training week. Missing one workout does not ruin anything - the thing that ruins progress is deciding the week is already written off and skipping the rest.
  • NO CARDIO IS PROGRAMMED You are on your feet all day already and your calories were set around that job, not on top of it. We are not going to double-count it. You did not give us a step count, so for the first week just wear your phone and we will find out what your normal actually is - then we set a target from YOUR number, not a generic 10,000. Cardio is a tool we hold in reserve: if your 10–14 day weight trend genuinely stalls on honest logging, and the tape and photos agree, a small amount of easy walking goes in before your food comes down again. It is never a punishment for a bad week.
  • SLEEP IS YOUR WEAK LINK Six hours with a very active job and four to five lifting sessions is thin. If sessions feel flat, look at sleep before you change anything else. Getting to seven hours will do more for how you look than any exercise swap.

Recovery + Optional

CARDIO PROTOCOL

You are on your feet all day at work. That already does the job most programs use cardio for, and your calorie target has been set around that activity rather than on top of it — so there is no cardio prescribed in Phase 1 at all, and no step target either — you have never counted steps, so inventing a number would be a guess. Week 1 we just look at what your normal work week actually is, and that becomes your baseline. Hit your four mandatory sessions, keep work activity normal, and let the food do the fat loss. Cardio is held in reserve as a tool for later: if the weight trend genuinely stalls for 10–14 days on honest logging, we add a small amount of easy work before we cut your food again. It is never a punishment for a bad week, and it never replaces eating properly.

NUTRITION

Science-based phased nutrition. Calories drop each block as your body adapts. Every meal, every day, every week — all 12 weeks mapped out.

THE SCIENCE
TDEE ~3,402 kcal (high job + training). Protein set at 2.05g/kg (~174g/day) per Morton et al. (2018) for hypertrophy. Deficit increases progressively as the program progresses to maintain consistent rate of fat loss. Carbs concentrated around training; pre-bed casein/cottage cheese supports overnight muscle protein synthesis.

The Other Stuff

LIFESTYLE

Training and food are 70% of the equation. The rest comes down to sleep, stress, and staying consistent when motivation dips.

😴
SLEEP
Target 7+ hours every night. You're averaging ~6 — this is the single biggest leverage point you have. Leproult & Van Cauter (2011) showed 1 week of 5-hr sleep cut testosterone 10–15%, and Dattilo et al. (2011) showed sleep restriction blunts muscle protein synthesis. A 1-hour sleep increase is worth more than any program tweak we could make.

  • Set a consistent bedtime — same time every night
  • No screens 30 mins before bed if possible
  • Magnesium Glycinate before bed will genuinely help
  • Keep your room cool and dark
🧠
STRESS
Moderate stress is normal — what matters is recovery between hits. A few habits can keep cortisol in check across the week.

  • Training itself is one of the best stress relief tools — use it
  • A 10-minute walk after work helps the nervous system shift gears
  • 10 mins of deep breathing or a walk after work makes a difference
  • Don't skip rest days — they matter
📸

  • How your clothes fit (especially waist)
  • Strength improvements week to week
  • Energy levels throughout the day

  • Sleep quality out of 10
